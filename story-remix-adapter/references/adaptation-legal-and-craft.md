# Adaptation Legal And Craft Reference

## Copyright-Aware Adaptation Principles

Use this as creative risk guidance, not legal advice.

Primary legal anchors:

- The U.S. Copyright Office states that copyright protects expression, not ideas, procedures, methods, systems, processes, concepts, principles, or discoveries: https://www.copyright.gov/what-is-copyright/
- The U.S. Copyright Office FAQ similarly says facts, ideas, systems, and methods are not protected, although their expression can be: https://www.copyright.gov/help/faq/faq-general.html
- Copyright owners have the exclusive right to prepare derivative works based on the copyrighted work. See Copyright Office Circular 1: https://www.copyright.gov/circs/circ01.pdf
- Fair use is fact-specific. The Copyright Office notes that transformative uses are more likely to be fair, and market substitution/harm matters: https://www.copyright.gov/fls/fl102.html

Practical rule for commercial short-drama adaptation:

- Do not rely on "I changed the names" or "I changed ancient to modern."
- Do not make an unauthorized derivative of a recognizably specific plot expression.
- Extract broad mechanisms and rebuild a new expression from the ground up.

## What Can Be Extracted

Generally safer abstract mechanisms:

- "A desperate parent needs money to save a child."
- "A betrayed worker finds a hidden resource and rises."
- "A secret rule in a dangerous place rewards competence and punishes greed."
- "A fair buyer changes the protagonist's fate."
- "Public success later exposes the truth of the protagonist's suffering."

These are not enough by themselves; rebuild characters, setting, rules, scenes, objects, dialogue, and payoffs.

## What Must Be Replaced

High-risk elements to avoid copying:

- Exact names and relationship map.
- Exact sequence of scenes.
- Distinctive props, formulas, secret verses, coordinates, maps, tokens, artifacts, system prompts, or rules.
- Iconic humiliation scene details.
- Exact prices, numbers, medical setup, transaction beats, and public speech structure when they are distinctive in combination.
- Dialogue phrasing, catchphrases, jokes, insults, threats, and emotional confession wording.
- Unique villain method and climax mechanism.

## Attraction Mechanism Extraction

Ask:

- What pain hooks the viewer in the first minute?
- What unfairness creates anger?
- What competence fantasy gives pleasure?
- What secret knowledge or hidden rule makes the protagonist special?
- What moral contrast makes the villain satisfying to defeat?
- What public proof scene makes the audience feel vindicated?

Then convert the answers into a different domain.

Example conversion:

- Source mechanism: impoverished mountain gatherer uses inherited mountain knowledge to find wild ginseng and save daughter.
- New domain option: laid-off shipyard mechanic uses grandfather's tide notebooks to salvage a lost medicinal coral culture and save a clinic child.
- New domain option: rural tea-roaster deciphers old kiln records to recover a rare fire-cured tea process and breaks a county distributor monopoly.
- New domain option: desert courier follows ancestral well maps to find a buried seed vault and saves a drought village.

## Worldview Mapping Template

Use a table:

| Source Function | Source Expression | New Function | New Expression |
|---|---|---|---|
| Rescue stakes | Child illness | Urgent human stakes | Different medical/social crisis |
| Hidden resource | Wild ginseng valley | Rare value source | New industry-specific resource |
| Secret rule | Mountain verse/compass | Competence rule | Different inherited method |
| Betrayal | In-law steals profit | Trust breach | Different relationship and power imbalance |
| Fair buyer | Medicine expert | Legitimizing authority | Different professional gatekeeper |
| Public vindication | Entrepreneur award speech | Truth arena | Different public reckoning |

## Originality Distance Checklist

Pass only if most answers are "yes":

- Would a reader summarize the new work with a different logline?
- Could the protagonist solve problems only because of the new world and skill?
- Are the major scenes different in location, action, and visual design?
- Are the rules newly planted and paid off?
- Are the antagonist's tools different?
- Is the ending arena different?
- Are all dialogue lines newly written?
- Would the new work avoid functioning as a market substitute for the source?

## Self-Correction Loop

If the draft feels close to the source:

1. Identify the copied function.
2. Identify the copied expression.
3. Keep the function only if it is an abstract audience mechanism.
4. Replace the expression with a new domain-specific event.
5. Update downstream causality so the change matters.
6. Rerun the originality checklist.

## Minimum Final Report

Include:

- `吸引力机制`: 2-5 bullets.
- `核心替换`: table of old function to new expression.
- `侵权风险自查`: pass/fail bullets.
- `评分`: 90+ only after revision.
